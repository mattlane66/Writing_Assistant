# Text-world coherence playbook

## Purpose

Fluent sentences can describe a world that does not add up. This playbook adds a consistency layer to the editorial and argument methods: construct a compact representation of what the passage establishes, update it as the passage proceeds, and test later statements against earlier commitments.

The method borrows one useful idea from AI world-model research without claiming that this application contains a separate embodied world-model architecture. In that research, an agent compresses observations into an internal state and predicts how the environment changes over time or in response to actions. Ha and Schmidhuber describe policies trained inside an environment generated by an internal model; LeCun frames prediction of future states as a basis for reasoning and planning. For writing analysis, the analogous operation is textual and explicit: model the entities, rules, and transitions asserted by the draft, then look for incompatible states or missing transitions.

Primary methodological references:

- David Ha and Jürgen Schmidhuber, ["Recurrent World Models Facilitate Policy Evolution" (NeurIPS 2018)](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2018/hash/2de5d16682c3c35007e4e92982f1a2ba-Abstract.html).
- Yann LeCun, ["A Path Towards Autonomous Machine Intelligence" (2022 position paper)](https://openreview.net/forum?id=BZ5a1r-kVsf).
- The user's framing reference, [Wikipedia's overview of AI world models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_model_(artificial_intelligence)), is useful orientation but is not treated as primary authority.

## The text-world ledger

Build only the fields needed for the passage. Do not expose this ledger unless analysis requires it.

| Field | Track | Typical failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Entity | stable identity, aliases, kind, attributes, ownership, relationships | a person, object, institution, or term silently becomes another |
| State | present condition and what changes it | something is broken, empty, closed, dead, absent, or completed and later behaves as though unchanged |
| Time | dates, ages, duration, order, tense, simultaneity | effect precedes cause; elapsed time, age, or chronology cannot work |
| Space | location, direction, containment, distance, access, visibility | a character or object moves without time or path; a locked or hidden thing is accessed without explanation |
| Quantity | counts, totals, units, proportions, ranges, comparisons | parts exceed the whole; units change; numbers or denominators conflict |
| Causality | actor, action, precondition, mechanism, effect, alternative cause | a result lacks a possible cause; correlation is narrated as mechanism; responsibility disappears |
| Knowledge | who perceived, learned, remembered, inferred, concealed, or could know something | a character or narrator uses information with no acquisition path |
| Goal and choice | objective, constraint, available action, tradeoff, consequence | a decision contradicts its stated goal without explanation |
| Rule | definition, policy, scientific condition, fictional law, exception | a term or world rule changes meaning only when convenient |
| Reference | pronoun, demonstrative, comparison, quotation, source | a reference has no stable antecedent or comparison class |

## Sequential audit

1. Extract the minimum initial state: who and what exist, when and where, under which rules.
2. Read each material sentence as an event, observation, claim, or rule.
3. Apply its changes to the ledger.
4. Check its preconditions. Could the actor do this? Could the observer know it? Is the object available and in the right state? Do the units and totals match?
5. Check its consequences. What must now be true, false, possible, or no longer possible?
6. Compare the next sentence with the updated state.
7. Preserve unresolved alternatives when the passage supports more than one coherent model.

This is not a demand for exhaustive simulation. Audit at the resolution the writing needs: a two-sentence email may need only actor/action/deadline; a narrative may need a timeline, object states, character knowledge, and fictional rules; a policy argument may need causal and quantitative constraints.

## Inconsistency classes

### Direct contradiction

The same entity has mutually exclusive properties at the same relevant time and under the same interpretation. Before flagging, check whether time, perspective, modality, quotation, or changed circumstances reconcile the statements.

### Transition gap

Two states could both be true, but the change between them is absent or impossible under the supplied constraints. The repair may be a missing event, elapsed time, actor, mechanism, or explicit exception. Do not invent which one occurred.

### Temporal inconsistency

Dates, ages, durations, tense, or sequence conflict. Normalize relative expressions only when the reference time is clear. Distinguish narrative reordering from chronological contradiction.

### Spatial and physical inconsistency

Locations, access, visibility, containment, direction, object persistence, or ordinary physical preconditions conflict. Apply ordinary expectations only when the genre has not established different rules.

### Quantitative inconsistency

Counts, totals, denominators, percentages, units, ranges, and comparisons fail to reconcile. Recompute before flagging and preserve stated rounding or uncertainty.

### Causal inconsistency

The described cause occurs too late, lacks a necessary mechanism, conflicts with another asserted cause, or cannot produce the claimed scope of effect. Distinguish a missing causal bridge from a deductive contradiction and from an externally unverified causal premise.

### Knowledge-state inconsistency

A person acts on, reports, or reacts to information the passage has not made available to them. Check point of view, offstage time, inference, institutional role, and unreliable narration before calling it an error.

### Definitional or category drift

A key word, population, metric, standard, or comparison class changes midstream. This overlaps with equivocation in argument analysis but also appears in technical explanation and narrative description.

### Goal or motivation inconsistency

A choice conflicts with an established goal, value, fear, or constraint. People can act irrationally or ambivalently; inconsistency becomes an editorial problem only when the passage presents the behavior as explained but supplies no intelligible change, conflict, or tradeoff.

## Calibrating the finding

Use the least accusatory accurate label:

- `Inconsistent:` commitments cannot jointly hold.
- `Transition missing:` both states are possible, but the change is not supplied.
- `Unclear:` more than one coherent reading exists and syntax does not select one.
- `Needs verification:` the passage is internally coherent but depends on an external fact.
- `Deliberate or genre-supported:` the deviation appears purposeful under local rules.

Never use common sense as an excuse to overwrite the text. Common sense supplies candidate preconditions and consequences; the passage and genre determine which apply. A surprising event is not an inconsistency. A coincidence is not impossible. A person's change of mind is not a contradiction if the writing represents a change. A fantasy rule need not match physics, but it must remain legible enough for the reader to understand when it applies.

## Mode behavior

- **Proofread:** Do not redesign content. Correct only obvious mechanical consistency errors such as a mistyped name or number when one reading is unmistakably established; otherwise flag if notes are allowed.
- **Edit:** Repair local contradictions only when the intended state is clear from the passage. Preserve or flag material alternatives.
- **Rewrite:** Reorder or rebuild for coherent state transitions, but do not invent the missing event, cause, motive, or fact.
- **Compress:** Keep every premise, transition, exception, and qualification needed for the text world to remain intelligible.
- **Draft:** Establish enough entities, rules, causes, and transitions for the supplied material to make sense. Use a placeholder instead of fabricating a missing fact.
- **Analyze:** Name the precise commitments that conflict, classify the problem, show why it matters, and state the smallest information or revision needed to resolve it. Include argument analysis only when reasons support a conclusion.

## Final consistency questions

- Is each entity still the same entity unless a change is stated?
- Can the state sequence occur in the stated order and duration?
- Can each actor reach, perceive, know, own, or use what the prose says they do?
- Do counts, units, percentages, comparisons, and defined terms remain stable?
- Does every material effect have a possible cause or an intentionally withheld cause?
- Are exceptions, flashbacks, hypothetical claims, quotations, metaphors, and viewpoint shifts marked well enough to prevent a false contradiction?
- Does the passage follow its own genre and world rules?
- If something cannot be resolved, has the assistant preserved rather than invented the missing bridge?
