# Canonical writing primitives

These are the only peer-level primitives used by Writing Diagnostic.

## Seeing

### Perception
**Question:** What has actually been noticed?

Tests whether the writer has looked closely enough to distinguish the thing from a generic version of it. Flags language that substitutes assumption, vague evaluation, or abstraction for observation.

### Reference
**Question:** What exactly does this word point to?

Tests pronouns, generic nouns, antecedents, scope, and terminology. A broad word is not automatically bad; it fails when the intended object cannot be recovered.

### Particularity
**Question:** Has the thing been named as specifically as the writer actually knows it?

Tests whether an exact object, action, condition, role, measurement, or term is available and useful. Do not invent particulars to satisfy this check.

### Distinction
**Question:** Are meaningful differences being kept separate?

Tests collapsed categories, unearned equivalences, and evaluative words such as wrong, better, successful, or problem when the relevant criterion or subtype matters.

## Truth

### Agency
**Question:** Who or what actually acts?

Tests agentless phrasing, evasive passives, abstractions that appear to act, and concealed responsibility. Passive voice can pass when the agent is irrelevant or information order improves.

### Support
**Question:** What warrants this claim?

Tests observation, evidence, experience, reasoning, and grounds. Flags invented motives, unsupported judgments, and conclusions with no visible basis.

### Calibration
**Question:** Does the strength of the language match what is known?

Tests certainty, universality, precision, causality, comparison, and rhetorical force. A strong word passes when its scope is explicitly bounded and supported.

## Reasoning

### Relation
**Question:** What relationship is being asserted?

Tests cause, condition, sequence, contrast, comparison, dependency, warrant, means, and consequence. Connective language must not substitute for reasoning.

### Mechanism
**Question:** By what process does one thing produce or affect another?

A focused reasoning check for verbs such as drives, enables, supports, improves, creates, or leads to when the causal middle matters.

### Implication
**Question:** What does the sentence say, not say, and cause the reader to infer?

Tests hidden assumptions, unintended meanings, unsupported implications, and over-explanation of implications already available to the reader.

## Making the sentence

### Construction
**Question:** Was this sentence consciously made for this thought, or did a familiar shape volunteer itself?

Tests ready-made professional or academic constructions, clichés, habitual transitions, and syntax that appears to precede the thought. Familiar syntax may pass if it remains the best construction after testing.

### Necessity
**Question:** Does every word, phrase, sentence, and example earn its place?

Tests clutter, duplicated meaning, needless modifiers, throat-clearing, redundant transitions, explanatory residue, and removable material. Do not cut needed reasoning, qualification, evidence, or rhythm.

### Function
**Question:** What work is this unit doing?

Tests whether a word, sentence, or example observes, distinguishes, claims, qualifies, connects, demonstrates, questions, advances, or otherwise performs necessary work.

## Making the piece

### Architecture
**Question:** Is the thinking arranged in the order it requires?

Tests sequence across sentences and paragraphs, missing reasoning, buried premises, misplaced qualifications, and structural problems that line editing cannot fix.

### Emphasis
**Question:** Is the right thing receiving the right amount of weight?

Tests proportion, climactic language, buried importance, sentence endings, and whether every idea is being given the same rhetorical force.

### Distinctness
**Question:** Does the prose reflect this writer's actual attention, judgment, selection, and way of seeing?

Tests generic professional prose and interchangeable polish. Do not manufacture personality or unusualness.

### Reader fit
**Question:** What does this reader need made explicit, and what can safely remain implicit?

Tests missing context, over-explanation, unnecessary signposting, unexplained terminology, genre, and purpose.

## Governing test

### Intellectual honesty
**Question:** Is the language revealing the thought, or helping conceal its weaknesses?

Tests euphemism, abstraction, vagueness, false certainty, tidy resolution, suppressed uncertainty, invented vividness, and fluent language that makes unfinished thinking appear finished.

Intellectual honesty is a final adversarial check. Do not infer dishonesty merely because a phrase is abstract or imprecise.
